    Part 4: Edge Flip

    Implementation Detail

    The goal is to convert two adjacent triangles \((a, b, c)\) and \((c, b, d)\) sharing edge \((b, c)\) into a new pair of triangles \((a, d, c)\) and \((a, b, d)\) sharing the flipped edge \((a, d)\). Here’s a diagram showing the local neighborhood of the edge before and after the flip, with all variables labeled.

    1. I first check if the target edge e0 is a boundary edge using e0->isBoundary(). If true, the function returns e0 immediately and does nothing, since we cannot flip boundary edges.
    2. Before making any changes, I iterate through the mesh starting from e0's half-edge and use the next pointers to collect every single element in the two triangles. I gathered:
      • 6 inner half-edges: \(h_0\) to \(h_5\)
      • 4 outer twin half-edges: \(h_{1t}, h_{2t}, h_{4t}, h_{5t}\)
      • 4 vertices: \(a, b, c, d\)
      • 5 edges: The center edge \(e_0\) and four outer edges \(e_1\) to \(e_4\)
      • 2 faces: \(f_0\) and \(f_1\)

    3. Next, I reassigned the pointers to form the new geometry:

      • Face \(f_0\) becomes triangle \((a, d, c)\). Its new half-edge loop is: \(h_0(a \rightarrow d) \rightarrow h_5(d \rightarrow c) \rightarrow h_1(c \rightarrow a)\).
      • Face \(f_1\) becomes triangle \((d, a, b)\). Its new half-edge loop is: \(h_3(d \rightarrow a) \rightarrow h_2(a \rightarrow b) \rightarrow h_4(b \rightarrow d)\).

      I used the setNeighbors(next, twin, vertex, edge, face) function to update all 5 pointers for each of the 6 inner half-edges at once. Unchanged Elements: The twin pointers for \(h_1, h_2, h_4, h_5\) remain exactly the same, because their connections to the outside triangles are not affected by the local flip.

    4. Finally, to ensure total consistency, update every vertex, edge, and face pointers back to a valid half-edge that belongs to it.

    Implementation tricks

    For this part, I did not experience a difficult debugging journey. My main trick was doing all the work on scratch paper before typing any code.

    By drawing out the two triangles(like the graph above), explicitly labeling every single half-edge, vertex, edge, and face with variable names, and mapping out their relationships before and after the flip, I avoided confusion. Translating this visual diagram into the setNeighbors calls made the code work correctly on the first try.
